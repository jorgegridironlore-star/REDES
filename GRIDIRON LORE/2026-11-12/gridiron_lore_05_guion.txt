The longest kick in NFL history, for forty-three years, came from a man born without toes.
Tom Dempsey was born with no toes on his right foot, and no fingers on his right hand.
He kicked anyway, in a special shoe with a flat, squared-off front.
November eighth, nineteen seventy. Tulane Stadium, New Orleans.
Saints against Lions.
Detroit leads seventeen to sixteen. Two seconds left.
The holder kneels at the Saints' own thirty-seven-yard line.
That's a sixty-three-yard field goal.
The NFL record at the time? Fifty-six yards.
Nobody in the stadium thought it was possible.
Dempsey swung that flat shoe, and the ball just kept going.
It cleared the crossbar as time expired. Saints nineteen, Lions seventeen.
He had broken the record by seven yards.
Some people said the shoe was an unfair advantage.
In nineteen seventy-seven, the league changed the rules for kicking shoes. It's known as the Tom Dempsey Rule.
His record stood for forty-three years.
Today, the longest field goal is sixty-eight yards, by Cam Little, in twenty twenty-five.
But it all started with one flat shoe in New Orleans.
