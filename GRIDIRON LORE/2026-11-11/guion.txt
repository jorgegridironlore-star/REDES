The coldest game in NFL history was so cold, the referees couldn't use their whistles.
December thirty-first, nineteen sixty-seven. Lambeau Field, Green Bay.
The NFL Championship. Packers against Cowboys.
At kickoff, it was thirteen degrees below zero.
With the wind, it felt like minus forty or worse.
The field had an underground heating system. It failed. The turf froze solid, like an ice rink.
On the opening kickoff, the officials' metal whistles froze to their lips.
When one referee pulled his away, it tore the skin.
So for the rest of the game, the officials just yelled "Stop!"
The halftime show? Cancelled. The band's instruments froze, and several members were treated for hypothermia.
More than fifty thousand fans stayed anyway.
Dallas led seventeen to fourteen in the final minutes.
Green Bay drove down the frozen field, inch by inch.
Third down, at the one-yard line, seconds left, no timeouts.
A field goal would tie it. Lombardi went for the win.
Bart Starr kept the ball himself, and dove behind his guard into the end zone.
Packers twenty-one, Cowboys seventeen.
Green Bay's third straight title, won in a game where nobody could blow a whistle.
