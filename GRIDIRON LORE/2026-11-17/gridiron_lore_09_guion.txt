In forty years, Pittsburgh had never won a playoff game. Then a ball bounced off a collision.
December twenty-third, nineteen seventy-two. Three Rivers Stadium, Pittsburgh.
A playoff game. Raiders against Steelers.
Ken Stabler scrambles thirty yards for a touchdown. Raiders seven, Steelers six.
Twenty-two seconds left. Fourth and ten, at the Pittsburgh forty. No timeouts.
Terry Bradshaw drops back, and the Oakland rush is all over him.
He escapes, and fires it down the middle for Frenchy Fuqua.
Jack Tatum arrives at the same moment. Bang.
The ball flies backward, toward the Steelers.
Rookie Franco Harris scoops it up, inches off the turf.
He runs down the left sideline, and slips past one last tackle at the ten.
Touchdown.
But who touched it? Back then, if the ball hit only Fuqua, Harris couldn't catch it.
Referee Fred Swearingen walked to a phone by the field.
Then he raised both arms. Touchdown.
Steelers thirteen, Raiders seven.
After four decades, Pittsburgh finally had a playoff win.
That night, a fan gave the play its name: the Immaculate Reception.
Fifty years later, nobody can prove who the ball hit.
