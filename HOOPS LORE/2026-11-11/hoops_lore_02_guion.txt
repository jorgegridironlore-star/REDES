The greatest scoring night in NBA history... and not a single camera filmed it.

March second, nineteen sixty-two. Hershey, Pennsylvania.

Not a big city arena. A concrete hockey barn in the chocolate town.

Philadelphia Warriors against the New York Knicks.

Only four thousand one hundred twenty-four fans were there. No television cameras.

And that night, Wilt Chamberlain scored one hundred points. One hundred. By himself.

Wilt was a terrible free throw shooter. That night, he made twenty-eight of thirty-two.

In the fourth quarter, the crowd started chanting: "Give it to Wilt!"

With forty-six seconds left, he dunked for point number one hundred.

Fans ran onto the court, and the game had to stop.

Final score: one sixty-nine to one forty-seven.

So how do we know it happened? A radio broadcast was recorded, and part of it survived.

And the most famous photo was taken in the locker room: Wilt holding a sheet of paper with "one hundred" written on it.

No video exists of the greatest scoring night in basketball. More than sixty years later, nobody has broken the record.
