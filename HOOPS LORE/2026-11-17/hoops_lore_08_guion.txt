Thirty-seven points in one quarter. And he never missed a single shot.
January twenty-third, two thousand fifteen. Oracle Arena, Oakland.
The Golden State Warriors host the Sacramento Kings.
In the first half, Klay Thompson makes just three of his nine shots.
Nine minutes and forty-five seconds left in the third. Klay hits a jumper. Warriors up sixty to fifty-eight.
Then he steals the ball and pulls up for three.
And he doesn't stop.
Three after three after three. And the Oracle crowd gets louder every time.
Over the last three minutes, Klay alone outscores the Kings eighteen to three.
Thirteen shots. Thirteen makes.
Nine threes. Nine makes.
Two free throws. Both good.
Thirty-seven in a single quarter. A new NBA record.
The old record? Thirty-three, by George Gervin and Carmelo Anthony.
The whole Kings team scored twenty-two in that quarter.
Final score: Warriors one hundred twenty-six, Kings one hundred one.
Klay finished with fifty-two, and sat out the last nine and a half minutes.
His coach, Steve Kerr, once played next to Michael Jordan. His verdict? I never saw him do that.
