The NBA once played an official game with the rims two feet higher.
March seventh, nineteen fifty-four. The Minneapolis Auditorium.
The Milwaukee Hawks visit the Minneapolis Lakers.
For one night, the baskets went from ten feet to twelve.
The target: big men, like Lakers center George Mikan.
Colleges were debating higher rims, so the NBA tested it first.
"Nobody could hit the darn thing," said Lakers coach John Kundla.
The Lakers made just twenty-eight percent of their shots.
Mikan missed his first twelve. He finished two for fourteen.
But the big guys still got the rebounds.
Mikan said it threw the whole game out of sync, and made it tougher on the smaller man.
Five-foot-ten guard Slater Martin wanted a six-foot basket instead. "It would make a Mikan out of me."
The Lakers won anyway, sixty-five to sixty-three.
That spring, they won the championship too.
The twelve-foot rim was never tried again in the NBA.
More than seventy years later, the rims are still ten feet high.
