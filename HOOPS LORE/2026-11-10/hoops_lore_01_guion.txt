In the very first NBA game... you got in for free if you were taller than this guy.

November first, nineteen forty-six. Toronto. The Huskies host the New York Knickerbockers, in the first game of a brand-new league. It wasn't even called the NBA yet. That name came three years later.

The court? Laid right on top of a hockey rink. The ice stayed underneath, the wooden floor got slippery, and the hockey boards stayed up around the players.

Selling basketball in hockey country was hard. Tickets cost seventy-five cents to two dollars and fifty cents. So the Huskies ran a newspaper ad: if you're taller than our center, George Nostrand... six foot eight... you get in free. You just had to prove it at the team office the day before.

Seven thousand and ninety fans showed up, for the first pro basketball game ever played in Canada. One Knicks player said the crowd treated the jump ball like a hockey face-off.

Ossie Schectman scored the first basket in league history. Nostrand put up sixteen points... but the Knicks won, sixty-eight to sixty-six.

The Huskies lasted one single season. The Knicks are still here.
