This NBA game went six overtimes, and in those thirty extra minutes, both teams scored just eighteen points.
January sixth, nineteen fifty-one. Edgerton Park Arena, in Rochester, New York.
The Indianapolis Olympians visit the Rochester Royals.
End of regulation: sixty-five, sixty-five. Overtime.
But back then, there was no shot clock.
So both teams did the same thing: get the ball, hold it, and wait for one last shot.
First overtime: two points each.
Second overtime: nobody scored. Not a single point.
Third overtime: two each. Fourth overtime: zero again.
Players just stood on the court, watching the clock run down.
Fifth overtime: four points each. Still tied.
Royals guard Red Holzman played seventy-six of the seventy-eight minutes.
Sixth overtime. Indianapolis holds the ball, one more time.
One second left. Ralph Beard shoots... Good!
Seventy-five, seventy-three. Final.
Fewer than four thousand fans saw the longest game in NBA history.
Games like this pushed the league to adopt the twenty-four-second shot clock in nineteen fifty-four.
Since then, no NBA game has ever gone six overtimes again.
