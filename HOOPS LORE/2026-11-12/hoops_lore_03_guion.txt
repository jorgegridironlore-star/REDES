The lowest-scoring game in NBA history ended nineteen to eighteen.
November twenty-second, nineteen fifty. The Minneapolis Auditorium.
The Fort Wayne Pistons visit the Minneapolis Lakers, the defending champions.
The Lakers had George Mikan, the most dominant big man in the game.
Fort Wayne's plan: keep the ball away from Mikan. So... just don't shoot.
There was no shot clock.
The Pistons held the ball. Sometimes for three minutes at a time.
Seven thousand fans booed and stomped their feet.
The Lakers scored one point in the entire fourth quarter.
Final seconds. Lakers lead, eighteen to seventeen.
Rookie Larry Foust puts it up, over Mikan's outstretched arms.
Fort Wayne wins. Thirty-seven points, in a whole game.
Mikan scored fifteen of his team's eighteen.
Stalling kept spreading. Basketball was getting boring.
Syracuse owner Danny Biasone pushed for a time limit on every possession.
In nineteen fifty-four, the NBA added the twenty-four-second shot clock.
The next season, scoring jumped by more than thirteen points per game.
Every possession today runs on that clock, because of a game nobody wanted to watch.
